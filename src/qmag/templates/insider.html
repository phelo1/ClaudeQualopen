Saturday unusual-options scan who took an unusual risk this week, and what might they know?

Every {{ ins.weekday|capitalize }} at {{ ins.run_time }} New York the daemon reviews the last {{ ins.lookback_days }} days of market-wide options flow from Unusual Whales: flow alerts with at least ${{ '{:,.0f}'.format(ins.min_premium) }} premium, volume ≥ {{ '%g'|format(ins.min_volume_oi_ratio) }}× open interest, ≥ {{ '%g'|format(ins.min_otm_pct) }}% out of the money, ≤ {{ ins.max_dte }} days to expiry and ≥ {{ '%.0f%%'|format(ins.min_ask_side_pct * 100) }} filled at the ask{% if ins.contract_screen %}, plus each session's unusual-contract screen{% endif %}. Index and ETF products are excluded. Each ticker's week is scored 0–10 for size, distance, urgency, conviction and whether the contracts expire before the next scheduled earnings; tickers scoring ≥ {{ '%g'|format(ins.min_flag_score) }} are flagged{% if ins.ai_enabled %} and handed to the AI analyst with the public context (headlines, earnings date, insider filings) for a strict-JSON read of the likely catalyst and what the buyer is speculating on{% endif %}.

How to read a flag

A flag is a research lead, not an accusation. Unusual, aggressive, short-dated, out-of-the-money positioning that expires before any scheduled event is consistent with someone acting on information the market does not have — it is also consistent with a hedge, a fund rolling a position, or a wrong guess. The AI reads only the facts shown here and is instructed to prefer the boring explanation whenever public information supplies one. Use what to check as the to-do list before drawing any conclusion.
